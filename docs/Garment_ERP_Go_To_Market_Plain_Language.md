# How to Sell the Garment ERP System in Thailand
### A plain-language guide to pricing and finding customers

*Version 1.0 — August 2026*

---

## What this document answers

You have built a software system that helps custom apparel and printing factories run their business — tracking orders, staff, materials, costs, and paperwork. Now you need to decide how to sell it.

There are three questions:

1. Should customers **buy it once** and install it on their own computer, or **pay every year** to use it over the internet?
2. **How much** should it cost?
3. **How do you find** the factories who will buy it?

This document answers all three in ordinary language. Prices are in Thai baht and exclude VAT.

---

## Part 1: Buy once, or pay yearly?

### The two options explained

**Option A — Buy once (called "on-premise" or "perpetual licence").**
The factory pays a large sum one time. The software is installed on a computer inside their own building. They own it forever. This is how older Thai programs like Express are sold, so many factory owners expect this.

**Option B — Pay yearly (called "cloud" or "subscription").**
The factory pays a smaller amount every year. The software runs on your servers, and they use it through a web browser. If they stop paying, they stop using it.

### The recommendation: pay yearly, with a "buy once" version kept as a backup offer

Sell the yearly version to almost everyone. Offer the buy-once version too, but price it high enough that most people choose the yearly one anyway.

### Why yearly is better *for this particular product*

**Thai rules keep changing, and your software has to keep up.**
Your system handles payroll tax forms, social security contributions, withholding tax, and VAT. These rules change. VAT is currently on a temporary reduced rate that the government keeps extending. The Revenue Department wants all businesses on digital tax systems by 2028. Every one of those changes means work for you.

If a factory paid you once in 2026, you would be doing that work for free in 2029. A yearly fee pays for the work that keeps their system legal.

**You cannot support many separate installations.**
If fifty factories each have their own copy running on their own computer, and you find a bug, you have to visit or remote into fifty machines. If everyone uses one shared system you control, you fix it once and everyone gets the fix that afternoon.

**Steady income lets you keep building.**
One-time payments arrive in lumps and then stop. Yearly payments arrive every year and let you plan, hire, and keep improving the product.

### Why you should still offer the buy-once version

Some factory owners will simply refuse to put their employee salary records and customer list on a server they do not own. Others are in provincial areas where the internet drops out during the workday, and a system that stops working when the internet stops is useless on a factory floor.

You do not want to lose those deals. So offer a self-hosted version — but price it at roughly three times the yearly fee, **plus** a compulsory yearly maintenance charge of about 18–20%.

A warning here: Thai competitors advertise "no yearly maintenance fee" as a selling point. Do not copy them. Instead, show the customer the total cost over three years, where your yearly option usually wins.

## Part 2: The tax rule that decides your price

This is the most important thing in this document.

### What the rule says

In February 2026 the Thai government published a new decree. If a small business buys software from a supplier registered on a government list, **the government lets them count the cost twice when calculating their tax**.

The limits:

| Condition | Detail |
|---|---|
| Maximum spending that counts | ฿300,000 per year |
| Who qualifies | Registered capital not over ฿5 million **and** yearly revenue not over ฿30 million |
| Valid until | 31 December 2027 |
| Requirement | Supplier must be registered on depa's Thailand Digital Catalog |
| Covers | Both buying software outright **and** yearly subscriptions |

### What this means in real money

Imagine a small printing shop that spends **฿280,000** on your system in one year.

- Normally, they subtract ฿280,000 from their profit before tax is calculated.
- Under this rule, they subtract **฿560,000** instead.
- Depending on their profit level, that saves them roughly **฿42,000 to ฿56,000** in tax.
- So the software really cost them about **฿224,000 to ฿238,000**.

*(These figures are illustrative. Every business's tax position is different, and the customer's accountant should confirm the actual saving. I am not a tax advisor.)*

### Three things this changes

**1. Yearly subscriptions are not punished.**
People used to argue that Thai buyers prefer to buy once because of tax treatment. For this rule, that is not true — a yearly subscription qualifies just as well.

**2. ฿300,000 per year becomes your natural ceiling.**
If you charge ฿600,000 once, half of it falls outside the rule and the customer gets no extra benefit on that half. If you charge ฿250,000 per year for three years, every baht qualifies. Spreading the cost is worth more to the customer.

**3. You must register on the government list, or you lose on price.**
Competitors like Prosoft and Business Plus advertise this benefit loudly. A customer comparing you to them will see your quote as roughly 40% more expensive if you are not registered — even at the same price. Getting listed on the Thailand Digital Catalog is essential homework, not an optional extra.

### An important limit most vendors do not mention

The rule only applies to businesses earning **not more than ฿30 million a year**. That is a small factory.

A medium factory earning ฿50–300 million gets nothing from this rule. You cannot use the tax argument with them. You have to sell to them on a different basis: how much labour, waste, and mis-quoted jobs your system saves them.

## Part 3: What to charge

### What else is on the market

To know whether your price is sensible, here is what Thai and foreign factories currently pay:

| Product | What it is | Price |
|---|---|---|
| FlowAccount | Thai online accounting only | about ฿5,500 per year |
| Mac-5 Legacy | Thai small-business ERP | about ฿667 per user per month |
| Odoo | International ERP, popular with Thai SMEs | ฿600–700 per user per month, plus setup |
| Odoo full project | 15–30 staff company, first year | ฿200,000–800,000 |
| Printavo / shopVOX | Foreign software just for print shops | roughly ฿3,500–4,000 per month plus per-user fees |
| Microsoft Dynamics 365 | Large-company ERP | ฿1.5 million–5 million |

There is a clear gap in the middle. Simple accounting software is too cheap and does too little. Big international ERP is far too expensive and does not understand sublimation or DTF printing. That gap is where you sit.

### Suggested price list

| Package | Suits | Users included | Yearly fee | Setup fee | First year total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Workshop** | 5–15 staff, one printing method | 8 | ฿48,000–72,000 | ฿30,000–50,000 | about ฿100,000 |
| **Factory** | 15–40 staff, several printing methods | 20 | ฿120,000–180,000 | ฿80,000–120,000 | **about ฿280,000** |
| **Multi-site** | 40+ staff, two or more locations | 40 | ฿300,000–450,000 | quoted individually | quoted |
| **Self-hosted** | Owners who refuse cloud | 20 | ฿550,000 once | ฿150,000 | plus ฿110,000 every year |

The **Factory** package is deliberately designed so that the first year lands just under ฿300,000. That is not an accident — it lets a qualifying customer use the entire tax benefit with nothing wasted. In your second year they pay around ฿150,000, which also qualifies fully.

When you send a quote, show the customer the price *after* the tax saving, not just the list price. It is a much more persuasive number.

### Two pricing details that matter in Thai factories

**Give away scan-only accounts for free.**
Workers on the production floor who only scan a job card should cost nothing. If you charge per person, the owner will buy five accounts and the floor staff will keep using paper — and then your system has no real production data in it. Free floor accounts are how the system becomes genuinely useful and hard to remove.

**Quote yearly, not monthly.**
Offer a discount worth about two months for paying the year up front. This helps your cash flow and matches how Thai businesses budget.

## Part 4: How to find customers

### Do not try to sell "a complete ERP system"

Selling a six-module business system to a twenty-person shop is a six-month conversation, and the owner's honest reply is "Excel is free."

Instead, sell the answer to the two questions that keep a print shop owner awake:

> **"Where is my customer's order right now?"**
> **"Did we actually make money on that job?"**

That is your production tracking and your job costing. You can demonstrate both in fifteen minutes, including sending the customer an order update over LINE. Payroll, inventory, and reporting come later as add-ons — which conveniently also spreads their spending across several tax years.

### The best way to reach factories: sell through machine dealers

The companies that sell DTF printers, sublimation inks, and blank garments already know every custom apparel shop in Thailand. They already have a trusted relationship, and they visit regularly.

Partner with them. Let them bundle your Workshop package with a printer sale and keep 20–25% of the fee. For a small team, this reaches far more factories than cold-calling ever will.

### The second-best way: accounting firms

Small Thai businesses ask their accountant before buying anything that touches tax. The accountant is the gatekeeper. Recruit five to ten accounting offices and pay them 15–20% of the first year's fee for referrals.

This also solves a problem you already have: your Thai tax settings are currently marked "pending accountant confirmation." Partner accountants will confirm them for you, for free, because it is in their interest that the system is correct.

### Get three to five reference customers first

Before you try to sell to strangers, give the system to three to five real factories at a heavy discount or free, in exchange for permission to use their name and their numbers.

There are roughly 2,600 textile and apparel factories in Thailand, and more than nine in ten are small businesses. In a community that size, one owner telling another owner that your system works is worth more than any advertisement.

### Use the deadline

The tax benefit ends on **31 December 2027**. That is a real deadline, not a sales trick. It gives you a genuine reason for a factory to decide this year rather than next year.

But plan for what happens afterwards: from January 2028, your product effectively becomes about 40% more expensive for small customers overnight, unless the government extends the scheme.

## Part 5: What could go wrong

**Small shops may simply not be able to afford it.**
Thai apparel manufacturers compete against Vietnam, Cambodia and Bangladesh on price. Margins are thin. Some owners will say no because the money genuinely is not there, not because they do not see the value.

**Workshop customers will cancel.**
When a small shop's order book dries up, software is one of the first things they stop paying for. Expect this. Build your plan assuming the Factory-tier customers pay the bills, and the Workshop tier is mainly a way to find future Factory customers.

**Custom work will destroy your profit if you let it.**
Every factory will ask for "one small change." If you agree to build custom features for a fixed price, you will spend months on work you can never sell to anyone else. Either charge by the hour for changes, or say no and put the request on your product roadmap.

**Support in Thai, by phone, is expected.**
Thai small businesses will call you. Your price has to cover the time you spend answering.

## Part 6: What to do first

A suggested order:

1. **Register the product on depa's Thailand Digital Catalog.** Everything else depends on this. Without it your price is uncompetitive.
2. **Confirm the tax settings with an accountant** — payroll tax forms, social security, withholding tax, and VAT.
3. **Sign up three to five reference factories** at a discount, in writing, with permission to use their results.
4. **Build a fifteen-minute demonstration** covering only order tracking, job costing, and LINE updates.
5. **Approach three printer or ink dealers** about a reseller arrangement.
6. **Recruit five accounting offices** as referral partners.
7. **Publish the price list**, showing the after-tax cost alongside the list price.
